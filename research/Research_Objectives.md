# Research Objectives

## Primary
Evaluate how AI-driven predictive analytics and automation may contribute to operational efficiency and management decisions in renewable energy.

## Secondary
1. Map AI use cases in solar and wind operations.
2. Investigate renewable generation forecasting.
3. Explore predictive maintenance and downtime management.
4. Assess relationships between AI adoption and perceived efficiency.
5. Identify data readiness, integration, costs, and skills barriers.
6. Propose an evidence-informed AI adoption framework.

## Research questions
- RQ1: How is AI used in renewable energy operations?
- RQ2: Can machine learning improve held-out solar generation forecasts over a baseline?
- RQ3: What operational benefits and limitations are reported for predictive maintenance?
- RQ4: Which barriers and enabling factors affect adoption?
- RQ5: How should AI initiatives be prioritized?

## Testable hypotheses
- H1: AI adoption is positively associated with perceived efficiency.
- H2: Predictive analytics use is positively associated with perceived decision effectiveness.
- H3: Data readiness is positively associated with AI implementation success.
- H4: Perceived costs and skills gaps are negatively associated with adoption.

No hypothesis is confirmed before data analysis.
